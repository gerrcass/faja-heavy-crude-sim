The resource is not the constraint. The constraint is mobility, lift, water, heat, diluent and processing capacity.

## 2. Geology and oil character

- The East Venezuela Basin is a foreland basin south of the Caribbean–South America fold belt. Oil generated in the deeper basin migrated updip into shallow Miocene **Oficina Formation** sandstones ([USGS 2009](https://pubs.usgs.gov/fs/2009/3028/)).
- Reservoirs are fluvial, nearshore-marine and tidal sandstones. They are **unconsolidated**, porous and permeable, but heterogeneous: shale barriers and facies changes reduce sweep ([USGS 2009](https://pubs.usgs.gov/fs/2009/3028/); [SPE 69697](https://doi.org/10.2118/69697-MS)).
- The oil is **biodegraded**. Bacteria removed light ends, leaving a dense, viscous, asphaltic crude with sulfur, vanadium and nickel. Sulfur is around 4.3 wt% in one upgrader feed; TAN around 3.2 mg KOH/g ([NACE 2006](https://doi.org/10.5006/C2006-06586)).
- The belt is not oil sands mining. It is an **in-situ** resource: wells, pumps and steam, not shovels and trucks.

## 3. Cold production

- Development is dominated by **horizontal and multilateral wells**, typically with laterals on the order of 1–2 km, arranged on pads. Petrozuata drilled 101 single-lateral and 140 multilateral wells in Zuata between 1998 and 2001 ([SPE 78969](https://doi.org/10.2118/78969-MS)).
- Cold wells produce sand deliberately or manage it with slotted liners/screens. Sand movement can create high-permeability channels (wormholes) and foamy-oil drive; it also erodes pumps and fills separators ([SPE 79025](https://doi.org/10.2118/79025-MS); [SPE 184968](https://doi.org/10.2118/184968-MS)).
- **Progressing cavity pumps (PCPs)** are the predominant artificial lift in several Faja fields. They tolerate viscous fluid and sand, but are limited by elastomer temperature, differential pressure and wear ([SPE 165061](https://doi.org/10.2118/165061-MS); [SPE 191118](https://doi.org/10.2118/191118-MS)).
- Historical Cerro Negro vertical/deviated wells produced 200–600 bbl/d of 8° API oil at 2,000 cP reservoir viscosity with rod pumps and top-drive PCPs; ESPs arrived for higher volumes ([SPE 97889](https://doi.org/10.2118/97889-PA)).
- Cold production is cheap relative to steam but recovers only a single-digit-to-low-teens percentage of OOIP. That is why thermal EOR is the next step, not an optional extra.

## 4. Thermal EOR

- **Cyclic steam stimulation (CSS, "huff and puff")** injects steam into a well for weeks, shuts in for days to weeks, then produces the heated oil from the same well for weeks to months. Steam is typically 300–340°C. Recovery is roughly 20–25% OOIP ([steam injection literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_injection_(oil_industry)); [SPE 188651](https://doi.org/10.2118/188651-MS)).
- **Steam flooding / horizontal alternating steam drive (HASD)** uses separate injectors and producers. HASD was studied specifically for the Faja to improve sweep in thin, heterogeneous sands ([SPE 117689](https://doi.org/10.2118/117689-MS)).
- **Steam quality** matters. Conventional field generators deliver roughly 70–80% quality steam; high-quality pilots have tested 95–99% ([SPE 218657](https://doi.org/10.2118/218657-MS)). Higher quality means more latent heat per tonne of water.
- **Steam-oil ratio (SOR)** is the system-level efficiency metric: barrels of steam (cold-water equivalent) per barrel of oil. CSS projects commonly run in the 3–5 range; poor projects run higher. SOR drives fuel gas, water treatment and CO₂.
- Thermal EOR is not automatically SAGD. SAGD needs thick, homogeneous pay and continuous steam chambers; Faja reservoirs are often thinner and more heterogeneous. CSS, steam flooding and HASD are the relevant family. SAGD pilots exist but are not the default story.
- Steam generation is the largest operating cost and emissions source. Water must be softened and treated; produced water is recycled; fuel gas is burned in once-through steam generators.

## 5. Lift, gathering and blending

- A PCP is a positive-displacement pump: a helical steel rotor turns inside an elastomer stator, forming sealed cavities that travel up the pump. Flow is proportional to speed. It handles viscous, sandy fluid but is sensitive to temperature, gas and abrasives ([PCP literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressing_cavity_pump); [SPE 165061](https://doi.org/10.2118/165061-MS)).
- Wells produce a mixture of oil, water, gas and sand. A **three-phase separator** splits gas, oil and water; sand is removed upstream or from the separator bottoms. Gas can fuel the steam generators; water goes to treatment.
- Extra-heavy oil cannot meet pipeline viscosity specifications on its own. **Diluent** (naphtha or a light hydrocarbon) is blended in, often 20–35 vol%, to reach a pumpable blend. Cerro Negro also tested **downhole diluent injection** to reduce viscosity before the pump ([SPE 69433](https://doi.org/10.2118/69433-MS)).
- The blend is a system loop, not a one-way additive: diluent must be sourced, transported, blended, and later recovered at the upgrader. Diluent availability and price are first-order operating constraints.

## 6. Upgrading and export

- An upgrader converts extra-heavy crude into a lighter, sweeter synthetic crude. The common configuration is **delayed coking** (carbon rejection) plus **hydrotreating** (hydrogen addition and sulfur removal) ([delayed coker literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delayed_coker)).
- Delayed coking heats vacuum residue to about 480–505°C in a furnace, then soaks it in coke drums. Coke yield is roughly 18–30 wt% of resid feed. The drums are quenched and decoked with high-pressure water.
- One Orinoco upgrader (180,000 b/d design) took an 8° API feed with 4.3 wt% sulfur and 3.2 TAN, blended with naphtha, and produced a 32° API synthetic crude with 0.2 wt% sulfur ([NACE 2006](https://doi.org/10.5006/C2006-06586)). That single example is a useful anchor; do not present it as every upgrader.
- Export blends are designated grades. Venezuela's Merey blend is a ~16° API heavy sour grade; the exact gravity and sulfur move with blending. The site should say "roughly 16° API" and avoid false precision.
- Upgrading creates byproducts that must be managed: petroleum coke, sulfur, metals, and CO₂. It also consumes hydrogen and fuel gas.

## 7. System constraints to teach

1. **Mobility**: viscosity falls steeply with temperature, but heat is expensive and uneven.
2. **Lift**: the pump must move viscous, sandy, gassy fluid; speed is not free.
3. **Water**: steam production, separation and recycling form a water loop.
4. **Diluent**: the blend must meet pipeline spec; diluent is a circulating cost.
5. **Upgrader capacity**: the field can outrun the plant; coke and sulfur are real outputs.
6. **Emissions**: steam generation dominates the carbon intensity of thermal paths.
7. **Heterogeneity**: shale barriers and thief zones make average numbers misleading.

## 8. Claims to avoid

- "The Orinoco is oil sands that can be mined." It is produced in situ.
- "All Faja wells use PCPs." PCPs predominate in many fields, but ESPs, rod pumps and gas lift appear.
- "SAGD is the main Orinoco process." CSS, steam flooding and HASD are the relevant thermal family; SAGD is a pilot/edge case in thinner or heterogeneous pay.
- "Recovery is 45%." That is a USGS assessment assumption with thermal methods, not a current field average.
- "Venezuela has 300 billion barrels of reserves, so production should be easy." Proven reserves, technically recoverable resources and producible rates are different quantities.
- Any precise current production figure without a dated source. Keep current output qualitative or clearly dated.

## 10. Model calibration

The simulator is an explanatory systems model, not a reservoir simulator. Its public contract is:

- monotone in the directions physics requires;
- bounded to published ranges;
- transparent about assumptions;
- labelled "illustrative" wherever a number is generated rather than quoted.

The exact equations and constants live in the implementation and its tests. The brief supplies the ranges, not the code.
